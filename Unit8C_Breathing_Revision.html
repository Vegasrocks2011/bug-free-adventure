Three words that are not the same

Almost every mark lost in this unit comes from using the wrong one. Check which one the question is about before you answer.

BreathingAir moving in and out of the lungs. Done by muscles.
Gas exchangeOxygen and carbon dioxide swapping places at the alveoli.
RespirationA chemical reaction in every cell that releases energy.

The summative paper covers 8A and 8C together. 8C is 28 marks in 35 minutes.

8C1

The gas exchange system

Lesson 1 · Question 1›
State
nose / mouth→trachea→bronchus→bronchiole→alveolus
Also: the rib cage, the intercostal muscles between the ribs, and the diaphragm (a sheet of muscle below the lungs).
Describe

Trachea carries air down from the throat. Rings of cartilage hold it open so it cannot collapse.

Bronchi are two tubes, one into each lung.

Bronchioles branch again and again, getting narrower.

Alveoli are tiny air sacs at the ends. The only place gases move between air and blood.

Explain
Oxygen diffuses from the air in the alveolus into the blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveolus. Both move from where there is more of them to where there is less.
Evaluate

Feature → consequence → effect (same answer shape as villi in 8A6):

  • Millions of alveoli → very large surface area → more gas exchanged at once.
  • Wall one cell thick → short diffusion distance → faster diffusion.
  • Moist lining → gases dissolve before they diffuse across.
  • Wrapped in capillaries → blood carries gases away → concentration gradient kept going.
Vocab
tracheacartilagebronchus / bronchibronchiolealveolus / alveolidiaphragmintercostal musclerib cagegas exchangediffusioncapillaryconcentration gradientsurface area
Watch
  • Bronchus = one, bronchi = more than one. Alveolus = one, alveoli = more than one.
  • The lungs contain no muscle.
8C2

Breathing: how the muscles move the air

Lesson 2 · Question 2 · The hardest idea in the unit›
State
The lungs contain no muscle and cannot pull air in. The diaphragm and intercostal muscles do all the work; the lungs follow.
Describe
Breathing inBreathing out
Intercostalscontract; ribs up and outrelax; ribs down and in
Diaphragmcontracts, flattens, moves downrelaxes, domes back up
Explain

Learn it as a chain. The marks are in the joins.

Breathing in

  1. 1The muscles contract
  2. so
  3. 2the volume of the chest increases
  4. which means
  5. 3the pressure inside the lungs decreases
  6. so
  7. 4pressure outside is higher than inside
  8. therefore
  9. 5air moves in, from high to low pressure

Breathing out

  1. 1The muscles relax
  2. so
  3. 2the volume of the chest decreases
  4. which means
  5. 3the pressure inside the lungs increases
  6. so
  7. 4pressure inside is higher than outside
  8. therefore
  9. 5air moves out

Year 7 link: squeeze the same particles into a smaller space and they hit the walls more often, so pressure goes up.

Evaluate

Exercise: muscles contract harder and faster → bigger volume change → more air per breath.

High altitude: fewer oxygen particles in the same volume → less oxygen enters the blood per breath, even though breathing works normally.

Narrow airways: air moves less easily → more effort for the same volume.

Bell jar model: good at showing that pulling the sheet down increases volume and draws air into the balloons. Limited because the jar is rigid (a real rib cage moves), there are no intercostal muscles, and the balloons do not branch into millions of alveoli.

Vocab
contractrelaxvolumepressurepressure differenceinhalationexhalationmodellimitation
Watch
  • "The lungs suck the air in" scores nothing.
  • Contract means the muscle gets shorter. The diaphragm contracts to move down.
  • Don't join steps with "and then". Use so, which means, therefore, because.

Inhaled and exhaled air

Lesson 3 · Question 3›
State

Nitrogenabout 78%about 78%none
Oxygenabout 21%about 16%down
Carbon dioxideabout 0.04%about 4%up ×100
Water vapoursomemoreup
Temperaturecoolerwarmerup
Describe
Limewater turns from clear to cloudy with carbon dioxide. It goes cloudy much faster with exhaled air.
Explain

Oxygen falls because some diffuses from the alveoli into the blood, to be used in respiration.

Carbon dioxide rises because respiration in cells makes it; the blood carries it to the lungs and it diffuses into the alveoli.

Nitrogen doesn't change because the body has no use for it.

Only about a quarter of the oxygen breathed in is used, which is why rescue breathing works.

Evaluate
Interpret limewater or indicator results. To make results more reliable: repeat each reading and compare them, and use the same volume of air each time.
Vocab
inhaledexhaledcompositionpercentagelimewaterreliablerepeatpattern
Watch
  • Exhaled air is not "all carbon dioxide". It is still 78% nitrogen and 16% oxygen.
  • Air and oxygen are not the same word.
  • Say "about 16%". Say the hundredfold increase in CO₂.
8C4

Aerobic respiration

Lesson 4 · Questions 4 and 6›
State

Respiration releases energy from glucose. It happens in every living cell, all the time.

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (energy released)
Describe
Reactants: glucose and oxygen. Products: carbon dioxide and water. Happens in the mitochondria. Energy is used for movement, keeping warm, growth and repair, and building new molecules.
Explain

Mitochondria are where aerobic respiration happens, so cells that need lots of energy have more of them.

Breathing vs respiration: breathing moves air in and out using muscles (a movement). Respiration is a chemical reaction in every cell that releases energy (a reaction).

Evaluate
A muscle cell contracts constantly and needs a lot of energy, so it has many mitochondria. A cell that does very little needs fewer.
Extension

Anaerobic respiration (not enough oxygen)

glucose → lactic acid (a small amount of energy released)

Much less energy, because glucose is not fully broken down. Lactic acid builds up and causes fatigue. The oxygen debt is the extra oxygen needed afterwards to break lactic acid down, so you keep breathing hard after you stop.

Vocab
aerobicanaerobicglucosereleasedtransferredmitochondriareactantproductlactic acidoxygen debtfatigue
Watch
  • "Energy is made / produced" is marked wrong. Energy is released or transferred.
  • Respiration is not breathing.
  • Not photosynthesis backwards: different reaction, different place.
  • Lactic acid is not lactose. Oxygen debt is repaid after exercise.
8C5

The effect of exercise

Lesson 5 practical · Questions 5 and 6›
State
During exercise, breathing rate, breathing depth and heart rate all increase. Breathing rate = breaths per minute. Heart (pulse) rate = beats per minute. Recovery time = time to return to resting values.
Describe
Measure pulse and breathing rate, take repeats, calculate a mean.
Explain
  1. 1Muscles contract more
  2. so
  3. 2they need more energy
  4. so
  5. 3the rate of respiration in muscle cells increases
  6. which
  7. 4needs more oxygen and glucose delivered, and makes more CO₂ to remove
  8. so
  9. 5breathing gets faster and deeper, and the heart beats faster to carry O₂ to muscles and CO₂ away
Evaluate
Describe the pattern using numbers, spot anomalies, and conclude by answering the question. A shorter recovery time suggests a person is fitter. It does not prove it.
Vocab
breathing ratebreathing depthheart ratepulse raterecovery timefitnessindependent variabledependent variablecontrol variablemeananomaly
Watch
  • "The body needs more energy" is not the whole answer: say what is delivered and what is removed.
  • Pulse rate, heart rate and breathing rate are different measurements.
WS7

Analysing data and drawing conclusions

Lessons 3 and 5 · Questions 3 and 5›
State
Reliable: repeating it gives a similar answer. Anomaly: a result that does not fit the pattern.
Describe
Describe a pattern ("as X increases, Y decreases") then quote two numbers that show it. Calculate a mean, leaving out any anomaly.
Explain
One set of readings is not enough to be confident; comparing with other groups helps. An anomaly is left out of the mean, not deleted from the table.
Evaluate
Judge whether a conclusion is supported or goes further than the data. Suggest one improvement and say exactly what it fixes (never "be more careful").
Watch
  • "It went up" is not a description. Use the numbers.
  • A conclusion answers the original question.
  • Reliable and accurate are different words.
